01 · Executive verdict

The direction is sound. The layers are not yet doing enough work.

ADR-0151 made the right structural move: audience-first routes, concrete demonstrations, outcomes before ontology. Do not reopen that architecture wholesale. The next pass should remove repeated exposition, show one clear action per screen and make the diagrams read as explanations rather than displays of technical capability.

3
Audience pathways to retain

Examiners, the public, and policy/strategy, each with a working example.

1
Standalone page to seriously consider retiring

The nine-step #/how story. Salvage its useful parts elsewhere.

0
Ontology or legal-rule changes proposed

Presentation, navigation and copy only. Keep the trust boundary and source distinction.

Highest-value move: Keep the front page and three audience doors. Each audience gets a single recommended demonstration, an obvious second step and a compact “What else could this enable?” disclosure. Put technical proofs, source internals and full graphs one level deeper.

Good foundations already present: self-hosted fonts, a restrained paper/green identity, source-specific shapes, a distinct colour scheme for the concept families, source quotations, review-status labels and text alternatives. Build on these rather than introducing a new visual identity.

04 · Issue register

Prioritised findings for implementation

P0 = risk of misunderstanding or fundamental friction; P1 = high-value UX simplification; P2 = polish and validation. Filter the items, then use the file itself as Claude Code's implementation brief.

All findings
F01

Retire the nine-step standalone “How it works” story

P0
#/how · site/js/how.js, site/js/nerds.js, site/js/layers.js, site/js/app.js
Observed

“How the tool finds an answer” spends nine scroll chapters on one rose-variety search, beginning with 2,460 tiny passage squares. This makes search feel like the entire proposition, despite ADR-0151 deliberately shifting emphasis to the shared foundation and three audiences.

Concrete alternative

Keep a four-step “How the foundation works” micro-story on #/nerds: sources → structured ideas and rules → traceable explanations → tools. Move the detailed retrieval example into Ask's existing “Why these sources?” disclosure. Redirect the old route and anchors.

Suggested copy
“The foundation turns legislation and examination guidance into linked records that tools can read. Each record keeps its source and review status. Different tools can then explain, research or trace the same material.”
Acceptance check

A reader can understand the shared foundation in under one screen; no duplicate nine-step scroll survives as a primary destination. Existing URLs still reach useful content. No legal or provenance data is removed.

F02

Do not present quotation matching as proof an AI answer is correct

P0
#/ask, #/examiners, #/ · site/js/ask.js, examiners.js, home.js
Observed

The site says the answer has “every statement … checked against its quotation” and elsewhere that “Every statement shows its source”. A matching quote establishes provenance, not necessarily that the reasoning is entailed, complete or legally correct. The results page explicitly says most records and answers are not expert-reviewed.

Concrete alternative

Use “linked to a source quotation” for the feature, “quotation not found” for the alert, and preserve “AI-written · unreviewed”. If there is an independent entailment/faithfulness test, explain and evidence it separately. Do not imply that a checked quote equals expert approval.

Suggested copy
“See the passages behind each part of the AI answer. The tool checks whether quoted text exists in the source; it does not guarantee the answer is right. Please check the wording and context yourself.”
Acceptance check

Distinguish quotation verification, relevance ranking and legal correctness everywhere these appear. Existing model and safety behaviours remain unchanged.

F03

“See the consequences” overclaims what dependency counts can tell you

P0
#/direction, #/change, #/whatif · site/js/direction.js, change.js
Observed

The direction-setting hero says “See the consequences of a change before you make it”. The demo actually lists records and Manual passages that might need reviewing, and explicitly cannot establish behavioural or policy consequences.

Concrete alternative

Put the actual capability in the headline. Present linked records as an initial review queue, not a measure of legal impact, risk or effort. Keep the “no recorded dependency does not mean no impact” caveat.

Suggested copy
“Find what a legal change might affect.” Supporting line: “Start with the recorded links between legislation, guidance and legal tests. See what may need re-reading before assessing the policy consequences.”
F04

Green ticks make fictional legal assessments look independently validated

P0
#/public, #/customers/objection/FM-0001 · site/js/public.js, customers.js, diagrams.js
Observed

The PARIS LANTERN card uses green check marks for “assessment” conditions. The case panel says “recorded as met”. A member of the public may read the checks as the site endorsing the examiner's assessment rather than faithfully presenting an invented report's statements.

Concrete alternative

Use a neutral document/report symbol and a badge reading “Report says: met”. Reserve ticks for verifiable UI tasks, not legal judgements. Add a short line: “This restates the fictional report; this tool does not assess whether it is correct.”

UI label example
▣ Assessment · reported as met — “The marks are deceptively similar.” As stated in the invented examination report.
F05

The concept bow-tie shows too many equally prominent interactions

P0
#/concepts/GC-0168 · site/js/concepts.js
Observed

28 relationships surround a central concept; short names are ellipsised, directional arrow buttons are small, and some terms recur (two “likely to deceive or cause confusion”; three “condition of registration”). Relationship selection, navigation and “Show 4 more” compete.

Concrete alternative

Make a grouped, readable relationship list the default, sorted by practical role. Show the first three per group; group duplicate destination names with “2 source records” and expand without losing provenance. On selecting a connection, show the predicate, condition, source sentence and review status in one side panel. Offer “See as graph” as an alternate view.

Acceptance check

Every connection label is readable without hover; selecting a relationship never silently navigates away; “Explore this concept” is a separately labelled link; duplicate records remain accessible; no underlying relation is merged or discarded.

F06

Rename the audience labels that do not say who they are for

P1
Global header/footer · site/js/layers.js, site/js/app.js
Observed

“For direction setting” is a description of an activity, not a recognised visitor group. “For nerds” is friendly but marks useful evidence and technical material as a niche or joke. The current </> button amplifies that contrast.

Concrete alternative

Test Policy & strategy and How it's built (or Technical detail) with the three audience types. Keep route IDs, colours and content unchanged. If the owner's preferred ADR labels must remain, use the new text as clarifying subtitles instead.

F07

The homepage headline describes the technology before its benefit

P1
#/ · site/js/home.js
Observed

“Trade mark law and guidance, turned into rules and code” and “a structured model … which people and programs can read and check” require the visitor to infer the point. The main action “Discover what it might enable” also leads downward before giving them a destination.

Concrete alternative

Start with the shared value: “One shared model. Clearer trade mark guidance.” Then a one-sentence plain-English explanation and three audience choices: “I'm an examiner”, “I'm applying for a trade mark”, “I work in policy or strategy”.

F08

The animated section 44 quotation is too much for an opening illustration

P1
#/ · site/js/home.js, site/js/diagrams.js
Observed

The source-to-rule demo presents a dense, truncated statutory excerpt beside four compressed condition cards, “THEN/UNLESS” branches and a Replay button. Superscripts and ellipses make it hard to see exactly what changed during the animation.

Concrete alternative

Show a three-panel annotated transformation: the Act says → four conditions it describes → source you can check. Let the visitor open the full quotation, exceptions and fact/assessment detail. Default to a static, fully rendered frame; Replay is secondary.

F09

Eleven selectable future tools turn the homepage into a catalogue

P1
#/ · site/js/home.js
Observed

“Build it once, use it for every tool” mixes six current demos with five future capabilities in an interactive checklist. The controls look like task checkboxes, but only switch a technical description. Much of it repeats the three audience pages.

Concrete alternative

Replace it with a small “One foundation, three uses” diagram, one current demonstration for each audience and a single “Explore more possibilities” link per group. If retaining a selector, use clearly labelled tabs or cards, not checkboxes.

F10

Safeguards repeat so often that visitors stop seeing them

P1
Global status strip, audience hero notes, demos, About · site/js/app.js, home.js, public.js, customers.js
Observed

The status strip, multiple page banners, trust section, demo footnotes and footer repeat “AI-written”, “not tested”, “not advice”, “never decides”, and sometimes the complete source names. Long repeated warnings create visual noise and dilute the important distinction at the point of use.

Concrete alternative

Maintain a concise global prototype status, a context-specific warning where a reader could misinterpret output, and detailed status on the record/answer. Collapse secondary explanation under “What this means”. Do not remove legally required safeguards or record-level authorship marks.

F11

Future capability lists are longer than the working demos

P1
#/examiners, #/public, #/direction · site/js/examiners.js, public.js, direction.js, illustrations.js
Observed

There are six examiner futures, eight public futures and six policy futures. All are shown as similar-sized illustrated cards with “BUILDS ON” and “STILL NEEDS” text. This pushes the live demonstrations down and obscures what exists today.

Concrete alternative

Show three distinct future outcomes per audience, ranked by usefulness and readiness; place the rest in an expandable “Other possibilities (N)” panel. State the horizon as words, not colour alone. Keep all ideas in the source data or appendix, even if hidden by default.

F12

Examiner hero diagram is too abstract to sell the concept explorer

P1
#/examiners · site/js/examiners.js, illustrations.js
Observed

The small illustration has a centre box titled “section 43 ground for rejection”, with tiny counts and relationship verbs on either side. It asks examiners to learn the visual grammar before knowing the benefit.

Concrete alternative

Make it an annotated working result: “Section 43 objection” → “28 recorded connections” → “Read the exact source behind any connection”, with one example edge shown at legible type size. The full bow-tie stays in the demo only.

F13

Ask starts with five long example cards before the answer

P1
#/ask · site/js/ask.js
Observed

A dense introduction, long input example, five multi-line example questions and three “what happens” explanations share the first screen. The result, which is the reason to use the page, is initially below all of them.

Concrete alternative

Shorten the intro to two lines. Offer three example chips, a “Browse 129” disclosure and a small “How to check an answer” link. Once an answer is selected, scroll/focus to the answer heading and show source support beside each claim; keep method details collapsed.

F14

Six case cards, two objections and six steps create three decisions at once

P1
#/customers/objection · site/js/customers.js
Observed

The public walkthrough opens with three explanation tabs, six invented case tiles, an objection selector, a six-step control, and a dense condition panel. The first meaningful explanation begins below several choice layers.

Concrete alternative

Open one recommended case and its first objection immediately. Replace six case tiles with “Change example” in a drawer/select; offer “Compare another objection” only when relevant. Put “Step 1 of 6” and Back/Next alongside the actual explanation, with the case diagram remaining visible.

F15

The case illustration is also a control panel

P1
#/customers/objection/FM-0001 · site/js/customers.js, diagrams.js
Observed

The “Case at a glance” combines filing details, conditions marked as recorded as met, two exception paths, a process timeline and clickable condition buttons. It is simultaneously a diagram, status report and navigation system.

Concrete alternative

Keep a compact case summary and highlight only the condition discussed in the current step. Provide a separate “Show the full rule” action. Make the diagram's click behaviour explicit: “Select a condition to see the exact wording.”

F16

The application journey's branch reads like a linear certainty

P1
#/customers/process · site/js/customers.js, diagrams.js
Observed

Eight stages are laid out in a line with a dashed route to “Lapse”. “Acceptance notified and…” truncates in the viewport. The alternative path is not explained directly on the picture, so the journey can look like the inevitable path of every application.

Concrete alternative

Use a branching journey with explicit labels “May proceed to”, “If an objection remains” and “Possible lapse”. Show full stage titles in a side card or two-line node; allow the active step to keep its full heading. Add “This is one illustrative route, not a prediction.”

F17

The top-six provisions graphic needs its denominator and its meaning

P1
#/direction · site/js/direction.js
Observed

“Where the guidance concentrates” shows bars for section 43 (351), section 6 (112), section 44 (111), etc. The colours distinguish direct and indirect links, but users may mistake the lengths for importance, risk or the amount of affected legal work.

Concrete alternative

Retitle “Provisions with the most recorded links” and explain “Counts are linked records in this snapshot, not importance or risk”. Label each bar with the breakdown (direct / via Manual), not just the total, and provide a “Trace this provision” action.

F18

Make the “Guess first” interaction optional, not a gate

P1
#/change/TMA1995/s44 · site/js/change.js
Observed

Selecting section 44 brings up a large “How many records…?” overlay that blurs the results. There is a “Skip, just show me” control, but users investigating a real question must dismiss the lesson before reading the result.

Concrete alternative

Show the result immediately. Put the prediction quiz in an optional callout above or below it: “Want to try guessing before you reveal the number?” Keep its educational value without interrupting a task.

F19

The radial ripple and 500-page Manual grid are disproportionate

P1
#/change · site/js/change.js
Observed

The section 44 view uses colour-coded concentric dots to represent 21 direct records, 34 citing Manual passages and 90 secondary records, followed by a chronology, reasons list and a grid of every Manual page. The actionable review list is comparatively quiet.

Concrete alternative

Lead with a stacked bar and the three exact, non-additive numbers; provide “Direct records”, “Through Manual” and “Citing passages” as tabs. Move the ripple behind “Visualise links” and the 500-page heatmap into an “Explore all Manual pages” drawer or linked technical view.

F20

The what-if repeats its fictional premise and risks drowning the question

P1
#/whatif · site/js/whatif.js
Observed

The intro describes the invented GI register, a four-column warning repeats its limits, then “What this example assumes” restates the premise before five touch points. The useful questions and source passages appear too late.

Concrete alternative

Use one concise fictional-scenario banner, then the five policy questions in a vertical list. For the selected question, split “Existing practice: cited passage” and “Question raised by the scenario” into two readable panels. Leave the detailed provenance below.

F21

The full graph is the least legible possible starting state

P1
#/map · site/js/map.js, netlayout.js
Observed

The default opens all 164 dots and 357 connections, with only some names labelled. Two scrollable sidebars, zoom controls, filters and a separate right-card list surround the central graph. The visual is impressive but has no obvious first question.

Concrete alternative

Default to a guided focus: “Start with section 43” or “Explore a trade mark application”. Show its immediate neighbours and a one-sentence insight. Offer “Show all 164 ideas” as an explicit switch, not the starting view. Keep the current full graph available for technical exploration.

F22

The map's colour families and source shapes need an in-place key

P1
#/map, #/concepts, #/rules · site/js/map.js, concepts.js, app.js
Observed

Orange/teal/violet mean kinds of ideas; blue square/amber diamond/plum hexagon mean document source. These systems are correctly distinct in code, but the explanation is spread between sidebars, captions and tooltips. Dots also vary in size by mentions, which is easy to mistake for importance.

Concrete alternative

Add a compact fixed “How to read this” key near the visual: Colour = type of idea; shape = source; dot size = how often named in the Manual, not importance. Keep a text label and shape for every source. Do not repurpose these colours for audience branding.

F23

The rules page repeats its own explanation at three depths

P1
#/rules · site/js/rules.js, illustrations.js
Observed

Three introductory cards use schematic drawings, then detailed sections follow for connections, legal tests and process templates, including the full section 44 test already highlighted on the homepage. Some thumbnails resemble placeholder skeleton screens rather than real examples.

Concrete alternative

Use a compact tab/selector for “One connection / One test / One process” and show a real, readable micro-example for each instead of abstract grey boxes. Move raw, complete structures behind “Inspect full record”. If homepage retains section 44, let this page link directly to the full details without re-explaining the teaser.

F24

The standards page should be a reference, not another long tutorial

P1
#/standards, #/nerds · site/js/standards.js, nerds.js
Observed

The standards stack is interactive, but the page begins with many simultaneous controls and a very long RDF code excerpt. The For nerds hub also presents eight technical figures and a one-line contract before a reader decides what they need.

Concrete alternative

Start with “What is stored / How it is checked / How tools use it” in a three-box picture. Treat RDF, SKOS, SHACL, SPARQL and PROV-O as a browsable reference. Keep live code examples but collapse by default with a clearly labelled “Show an actual record” disclosure.

F25

Results and limits should begin with a verdict, not six anchors

P1
#/about · site/js/about.js
Observed

The page has an on-page index, a detailed 129-question comparison, owner decisions, authorship charts, source snapshots, limits and a second “How Ask works” account. Those are important facts, but the information hierarchy hides the overall evidence statement.

Concrete alternative

Lead with a short evidence box: “Search ranked above keywords (+7.9 points), below search by meaning (−4.1), on these 129 questions; this does not validate answers.” Then “What has not been tested”, “Authorship”, “Sources & methods”. Move internal owner decisions to an appendix and remove the duplicate search walkthrough.

F26

Small icons need a clear vocabulary, not more decorative variety

P1
Global · site/js/icons.js, illustrations.js, explorer.css
Observed

The hand-drawn line icons are broadly consistent but often too subtle at 12–16px, especially the tiny icons on cards and labels. A dot, branch, target or code symbol can be decorative without communicating where it takes you.

Concrete alternative

Standardise 20px navigation and 16px inline icons at ~1.8px stroke, with accessible text labels. Reserve book/source marks for evidence, network for concept links, route for process, branching arrow for change, shield for limitations. Avoid using source diamonds/squares/hexagons as ordinary navigation icons.

F27

Secondary text is too faint for comfortable prolonged reading

P1
Global CSS · site/explorer.css
Observed

--ink-3: #74807b measures roughly 4.10:1 on white and 3.76:1 on the paper background #f6f5f0. Those ratios fall below WCAG AA's 4.5:1 target for normal-sized text. Some tiny all-caps labels and pale diagram legends use that token.

Concrete alternative

Use a darker neutral around #53615b for meaningful small text. Keep the existing muted colour for decorative rules and disabled elements only. Audit every small caption, badge, hover state and label on its actual background; do not force the vivid orange/teal/violet data colours to carry text.

Acceptance check

Normal text ≥ 4.5:1, large text ≥ 3:1, graphical information and focus boundaries ≥ 3:1 where applicable. The recorded colour-blind distinction of the two semantic palettes is preserved.

F28

Test keyboard, focus, zoom and non-colour alternatives through every demo

P2
Global + site/js/map.js, concepts.js, customers.js, change.js
Observed

Several graph nodes are clickable shapes; the source code already provides text equivalents and some keyboard routes. A desktop visual inspection does not establish whether focus follows a changed step, whether a selected node is announced, or whether the two sidebars remain usable at 200% zoom.

Concrete alternative

Add explicit regression scenarios: keyboard-only case switching, map search and node focus, no keyboard trap in the quiz, correct selected-tab announcements, diagram text equivalent, visible focus and reflow at 200%. Treat this as verification work, not as a claim of an existing failure.

F29

Give deep demo pages a clear return path and progress context

P2
site/js/layers.js, site/js/app.js, all demo views
Observed

The main menu correctly highlights the parent audience, but deep pages such as #/concepts/GC-0168 or #/whatif/FW-0001/search do not have a compact breadcrumb near the title. “Where to go next” appears only at the foot.

Concrete alternative

Add a simple breadcrumb above deep titles: “Examiners / Explore a concept / Section 43”. Put a contextual “Back to examiners” text link near it, without duplicating the full nav. For multi-step stories, keep “Step 2 of 6” adjacent to controls at every scroll position.

F30

Readiness, authorship and evidence need separate visual treatments

P2
Global states · site/js/app.js, illustrations.js, explorer.css
Observed

“Working now”, “Within reach”, “Needs more data”, “Demonstration”, “AI-written · unreviewed”, and “Working · not measured” are all pill-like indicators. They answer different questions: availability, future dependency, authorship and evidence.

Concrete alternative

Use a small, stable typographic system: availability (solid dot and label), authorship (rust-outline stamp), evidence (plain text “Not tested” or “Measured on 129 synthetic questions”). Never use colour as the only distinction or a “working” badge to suggest accuracy.

05 · Visual language

Refine the palette without breaking its meaning

ADR-0148 deliberately separates audience colours, concept families and source marks; ADR-0151 adds audience tints. Keep that contract. The issue is how many colours are simultaneously presented and how subtly their labels are rendered, not that the core brand needs replacing.

Brand / action

#1b5549 for primary buttons and links. Deep green remains the anchor.

Audience wayfinding

Rose / green / blue tint their own section. Do not apply these to record types.

Concept families

Orange / teal / violet only when representing kinds of ideas. Pair with words.

Source identity

■◆⬢
Blue Manual square; amber Act diamond; plum Regulations hexagon.

AI authorship

Rust “AI-written · unreviewed” stamp. Never conflate with readiness.

Readable captions

Proposed #53615b for small meaningful text, replacing too-light --ink-3 where used as copy.

06 · Claude Code handoff

Suggested implementation order and guardrails

Do not change: ontology/, authored/, graph/, legal interpretation records, generated site/data/ contracts, the live-answer prompt, provenance rules, or source/review status semantics. The UX pass is presentation-layer work, with tests and text alternatives maintained. Follow CLAUDE.md and record an ADR only for a genuinely new owner decision or non-obvious design choice.

Phase 1 · Reduce misleading or blocking experiences

Phase 2 · Give each audience one clean first success

Phase 3 · Make the presentation system consistent

Recommended prompt to paste into Claude Code

“Read CLAUDE.md, docs/DECISIONS.md (especially ADR-0148, ADR-0150 and ADR-0151), and this HTML review. Treat it as an implementation backlog, not permission to change the ontology or legal records. Start with P0 items, implement in small testable batches, preserve old deep links and provenance text, then address P1. For each change, document before/after behaviour, affected site files, and the tests used. Where a recommendation conflicts with an owner decision or a pinned safeguard, flag it rather than silently removing the safeguard. Do not make network model calls or incur paid tokens.”

Definition of done

  1. Each visitor can reach a useful demonstration from the main overview in one audience choice and one clear action.
  2. Every complex visual starts with a takeaway and has a readable non-visual equivalent; its detail is available on demand.
  3. Every statement about legal conditions, source checking, readiness and AI authorship remains accurate, visible where needed and independent of colour.
  4. The public can distinguish the fictional examination report's recorded assessment from what the website itself has evaluated (nothing).
  5. No source, record, test, related matter, uncertainty, or review status is lost by the UX simplification.
  6. Desktop/laptop widths, keyboard-only use, 200% zoom and print/accessibility equivalents are manually checked.
